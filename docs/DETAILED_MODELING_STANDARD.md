# Exterior modeling levels and detailed reconstruction standard

Detailed modeling (细致建模) means reconstructing the identifiable architecture of a specific building from verifiable sources, then checking its silhouette, proportions, major components and their connections against those sources from multiple views.

## Scope levels

- **Ordinary modeling (普通建模):** represent the mapped outline, basic height, principal roof form and broad material character. Basic geometric correctness is required.
- **Optimized modeling (优化建模):** a simplified model that resembles the real building, balancing ordinary and detailed modeling. Match the actual principal proportions, massing layout, roof forms, entrances and identifying architectural features. Secondary components and small ornaments may be omitted to control complexity. Check resemblance against available real-world references at normal campus viewing distances. The reduction is in the depth of detail, not in the requirement to resemble the real building.
- **Detailed exterior reconstruction (细致建模):** reconstruct a specific building's key components and construction relationships from verifiable sources, then verify them through multiple source-to-model views, as specified below.

Optimized and detailed modeling both require resemblance to the real building. Detailed modeling extends the depth of construction, detail and connection reconstruction; optimized modeling retains the essential architectural identity with fewer details.

Error repair is a baseline obligation at every level. Correcting a defect or adding repeated generic frames does not by itself establish either optimized or detailed modeling. Triangle counts do not determine the level.

Measured/BIM reconstruction is a separate accuracy and deliverable scope requiring authoritative dimensions, drawings and corresponding interior/construction information. None of the three exterior modeling levels implies measured or BIM accuracy.

## Architectural requirements

1. **Massing and proportions:** locate and proportion the main volume, towers, wings, roof ridges, setbacks, arcades and courtyard voids using mapped plans, published dimensions and photographs. Separate different wall/eave/roof heights. A maximum building height does not establish the whole section.
2. **Building-specific features:** reconstruct the principal entrances, actual window groups and bay rhythm, buttresses, cornices, columns, screens and other defining features in their source-supported locations. Their shape and distribution must follow the specific building.
3. **Connected construction:** connect stairs to landings and doors; support roofs, canopies and elevated elements; model continuous returns and edge conditions. Resolve floating parts, gaps, unintended overlaps and coplanar flicker.
4. **Material character:** use source-supported material zones and restrained color/finish differences. Preserve the visible distinction between stone, brick, glazing, roofing and joinery.
5. **Setting:** connect the building to its entry paving, immediate walks, accessible grade and courtyard treatment. Keep actual voids and openings clear.
6. **Multiple-view verification:** compare the final exported model with available front, side and aerial references. Match camera direction sufficiently to compare silhouettes, component locations and relative sizes. Inspect a low angle and the opposite side for connectivity problems.

## Evidence and acceptance

For each building, record references, the features reconstructed, known dimensions versus estimates, source-to-model observations, and remaining issues. Use photographs to check the actual architecture, rather than treating a source URL alone as evidence of agreement.

A building may be described as a completed detailed exterior model only after all applicable architectural requirements have been reviewed and no major silhouette, proportion or identifying-feature discrepancy remains. If an important source view is unavailable or a defining feature is unfinished, record the model as provisional and identify the gap.

Window/frame counts, triangle counts, material counts, layer presence and geometry validity are useful production or technical metrics; none certifies architectural fidelity. A successful renderer test also does not establish resemblance.

Secondary dimensions that cannot be reliably measured may be estimated and disclosed. Do not invent conspicuous features to increase detail. Photographic interpretation is not a measured facade survey, and intricate sculpture may remain a documented simplification.

## Technical delivery checks

After architectural review, validate finite coordinates, valid/closed CAD geometry where solids are intended, physically open passages where required, stable surfaces, correct units/materials, export consistency, and bounded browser cost. Preserve building selection, camera behavior and download/offline entry points.
